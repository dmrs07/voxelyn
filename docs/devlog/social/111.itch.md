Somebody played Voxelyn blind. Three runs, no guide, no questions asked, and he got
measurably further each time. Then he wrote me the single most useful sentence anyone has
ever sent about this game:

> I didn't exactly know what the exact goal was.

Here is the thing. The goal is written down. It is on a screen he definitely saw, in the
Operator Induction, article two, set in the mining company's own bold voice: descend,
recover, extract, and cargo only clears once you get back out. We wrote that sentence
carefully. We were proud of that sentence.

He read it. It did not land. That is not a player problem, that is a design problem, and
the fix for "my paragraph did not land" is never "write a longer paragraph".

## The document already knew

The funniest part is that the induction file has been confessing this for weeks. There is
a comment sitting at the top of it saying that it is not a guided tutorial, that it stops
exactly where discovery begins, and that its job is to make the first descent legible
rather than to solve it.

It was right about itself. Reading about a dodge has never once taught anybody to dodge.
So instead of rewriting the briefing for the fourth time, I built the thing the briefing
politely refuses to be.

Meet the Training Operation: a short, in fiction, fully playable drill. Two to three
minutes. Five exercises. Move, fire, dash, take the Core, get back out alive.

>> IMAGE 1 · 111-briefing.png · The briefing, with AUTHORISE DESCENT as the stamp and TRAINING OPERATION as the second button

It shows up in two places and it is never mandatory in either of them. There is a
permanent button on the terminal, and the first time you read the induction it now ends
with two buttons instead of one. The big glowing stamp still authorises a real descent.
The quiet one under it starts the drill. If you open the briefing from the training button
and then change your mind halfway down the page, the stamp still sends you into the real
Vein, because the stamp means what it says and not what you clicked to get here.

## No second engine, and I am insufferable about it

Zero lines changed in the simulation. Not one. The drill is the real game with surgery
performed on it before the first tick.

The whole trick is a run configuration that already existed:

    sectorCount: 1, coreSectors: [1]

Read that as a sentence and it is literally the tutorial: this run is one sector long and
the Core is in it. Two accidents of the existing rules make it work for free.

First, the boss rule starts with "sector one never has a boss", so the pedestal is unlocked
from the first frame with nothing to kill for the privilege. Second, extracting with the
Core in sector one produces the exact same run outcome a real successful expedition
produces. Not a lookalike. The same one.

The best part is what that buys in the HUD. The objective line reads FIND THE CORE, and
the moment you lift the Core off the pedestal it flips itself to EXTRACT AT THE ENTRANCE.
That is the real HUD doing the real thing. There is no tutorial HUD, no special overlay,
no tutorial mode hiding in the rules anywhere. The player learns the actual instrument
panel because it is the actual instrument panel.

## Carving a course out of a real sector

The map is not authored in an editor, because I do not have one and I did not want one.
The game generates a normal sector from a fixed seed, and then the drill cuts into it
before anything starts moving. The boss playtest tool has been doing exactly this for
months, so the pattern was already sitting there with its receipts.

The course is a straight line, on purpose, because the order of the rooms is the order of
the lessons: entry platform, gallery, firing chamber with two stalkers in it, a corridor
with pillars to dash around, and the pedestal chamber at the end.

Everything not in that course becomes plain rock with its surface wiped. That is stricter
than it sounds, and it matters. The lazy version just fills the gaps, which leaves the
sealed rock holding all the ore and crystal the seed originally put in it. Crystal emits
light. You would end up with a sealed wall quietly glowing blue, advertising a place the
drill has just declared does not exist.

Then there was the seed hunt, which went great, thanks for asking. The plan was to find a
seed whose entrance sits somewhere roomy, so the course fits without touching the edge of
the map. I scanned a few hundred. There is no such seed. The generator puts every single
entrance flat against the border like a fire exit. So the course steps inward on its own
and a short vestibule connects the platform to it.

## The soft lock that would have been genuinely funny

The instruction layer watches the same event stream that feeds the renderer and the audio,
and hands back cues: show this banner, queue that toast. The rule inside it took a couple
of tries to get right, and it is this. Irreversible facts are checked as facts, repeatable
actions are checked as events.

Here is why that matters. The course has no doors. Nothing stops you from ignoring the
dash prompt, strolling into the pedestal chamber, and lifting the Core while the game is
still politely asking you to press space. If the Core step listened only for the pickup
event, that event would arrive during the dash step, get thrown away, and then the drill
would sit there forever waiting for a second Core to be picked up.

There is no second Core. The tutorial becomes unfinishable, which is a spectacular thing
for a tutorial to be.

So dashing is checked as an event, because you can always dash again, and the Core is
checked as a fact, because possession does not expire. Breaking the sequence now skips
ahead instead of locking up.

## What a robot accidentally proved

To take these screenshots I scripted a bot to play the drill, and the game beat it for
several rounds. The first attempt died at twenty six seconds, and my own death screen
coached me on the way out: melee is telegraphed, the windup sound arrives before the blow.

It only started winning when it stopped spraying and began reading the screen. Find the
red pixels, that is a stalker, put the mouse there.

Which is the part worth keeping. Aim assist in this game is a purchased upgrade, and the
drill runs the factory chassis on purpose, because that is exactly what a new player has.
Nobody gets help in here. That is why the two specimens wake up eleven tiles away instead
of nine, just outside their own aggro range: far enough that stopping to read the
instruction never costs you health.

## The bug I am most fond of

Two problems turned up in review, and I would have shipped both of them happily.

The first is my favourite of the month. Extracting empty handed certified the drill. Walk
four tiles out, walk four tiles back, press E, and the game hands you DRILL CERTIFIED for
having learned absolutely nothing. It congratulated you for skipping the entire
curriculum. Now an empty extraction gets its own outcome, in the same corporate voice, and
the main button turns into REPEAT DRILL instead of waving you off into a real descent.

>> IMAGE 3 · 111-not-certified.png · DRILL NOT CERTIFIED, with REPEAT DRILL as the primary button

The second was a ghost. With the completion form on screen the run had stopped but had
never been properly closed, so pressing Escape still opened the field menu underneath the
form, and the field menu helpfully took the shared options controls with it, leaving the
terminal's Options screen empty afterwards. The run now closes the instant the form
appears rather than when a button is finally clicked.

## What it refuses to do

The drill never blocks you, never unlocks anything, and pays exactly nothing. The
completion form says so out loud, because the fiction should stay honest: training yields
nothing, the Vein below is where the money is.

And when it does teach you, it teaches with the real thing. Same Core, same interact key,
same HUD, same extraction, same rules that will later decide whether an hour of cargo
banks or evaporates. Nobody practices on a toy version of the loop and then gets handed
the real one at the worst possible moment.

The button is on the terminal screen. It takes two minutes, and if it fails to land the
way the briefing failed to land, I would very much like to hear about it, in as blunt a
sentence as the one that started all this.
